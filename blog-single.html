How to plan a student’s belongings across a whole UK education.

From the first collection at a boarding house to the final shipment home, a student’s belongings make dozens of journeys. Planning them once, properly, saves money, stress and a great deal of rebooking.

In this article

  1. Start with the school calendar
  2. Record everything on the first collection
  3. Plan storage around what is actually owned
  4. Build in exeats and half-terms
  5. Plan the final journey early
  6. A simple checklist for parents

Most families plan a student’s belongings one term at a time: a courier at the end of the summer term, a storage unit found in a hurry, a different arrangement for each exeat. It works, just about, until something goes missing or a deadline is missed. A better approach is to treat a student’s UK education as one journey with a few predictable stops, and to keep a single record of everything they own from the very first collection to the very last delivery.

Start with the school calendar

Every school publishes its term dates well in advance, and those dates are the backbone of a good plan. Mark the end of each term, the start of the next, every exeat and half-term, and any dates when the boarding house closes completely. These are the moments when belongings need to move.

Share the calendar with whoever will arrange collections, whether that is a parent, a guardian or the school itself. When everyone works from the same dates, collection windows can be booked early and nothing is left to the final week.

Record everything on the first collection

The first collection is the best chance you will have to create an accurate record. Every trunk, suitcase and box should be labelled and photographed before it leaves the boarding house, with a note of anything fragile or valuable.

That record becomes the reference for every journey that follows. When a student asks for their winter coat in October, or a guardian wants to check what is held, the answer is a quick look rather than a guess.

The record you make at the first collection is the one you will rely on for years. It is worth getting right.

Plan storage around what is actually owned

It is tempting to rent the biggest unit available and fill it over time. In practice, students tend to own less than families expect, and paying for empty space every month adds up quickly over several years.

Storage priced per item means you pay for what is actually held. As belongings come and go, the cost follows them, rather than staying fixed at the size of a unit chosen in a hurry.

  • Keep a short list of what the student needs during term
  • Note anything fragile, valuable or oversized before collection
  • Review what is stored at the end of each school year

Build in exeats and half-terms

Shorter breaks are easy to overlook. Some students take everything with them, others leave their rooms as they are, and some boarding houses ask for rooms to be cleared. Check the house rules for each break and decide in advance what needs to move.

Where items do need to move, a collection before the break and a delivery afterwards can be planned at the same time as the main end-of-term collections.

Plan the final journey early

The last move, at the end of sixth form, is often the most complicated. Belongings may be heading to university halls, to a family home in the UK or overseas, or a mix of all three, and each destination has its own timings and, for international shipping, its own paperwork.

Start planning a term ahead. Decide what goes where, allow time for packing to export standard if needed, and confirm delivery dates before the student’s last day.

A simple checklist for parents

If you take one thing from this guide, make it this: agree a plan before the first collection, keep a single record, and review it once a year. The checklist below summarises the steps.

If you would like help putting a plan together, the MoveAssist team is happy to talk it through, with no obligation to book.

Before the first collection

  • Confirm term dates with the school or guardian
  • Agree a single contact for collections
  • List anything fragile, valuable or oversized
  • Decide what stays in storage over the summer
  • Note the final destination when school ends
